I had always loved cars, even before I could drive. I didn't learn how to drive until halfway through my 20s. I liked cars even before I got into motorcycles. As a kid, I'd sometimes buy car magazines from the railway station book stall when I went with my dad to book tickets. At the beginning it was mostly to looks at pictures of cool cars but I did read some stuff. For some reasons I was fascinated but the price list at the end of each magazine that listed the price of each car in the market.

Growing up we only had scooters in our house and that was the only thing I could ride. We had always been able to make do with scooters. Nobody in my house knew how to drive anyway. Car ownership was never considered to be honest, at least that I know of. But right around the end of 2020, something happened that turned car ownership into a necessity. My father had a stroke. He recovered decently well but it left him unable to ride a scooter or even sit on it. Now we needed a car. The purpose was to drive my dad around. But first I needed to learn how to drive.

## The Alto Chronicles

Lucky enough, a few years ago, my sister had bought a used Maruti Suzuki Alto to learn how to drive. And now that she had gotten another car, that Alto could be passed down to me. The plan was to learn on this car and eventually get a new one. Some time later, she sent over the Alto with a friend. I was very excited about this car and to learn how to drive. A friend of mine knew how to drive so I asked him to teach me. He agreed to help but suggested that I still join a driving school because that would give me the confidence to drive on the road and the custom car driving schools use, meant that the instructor could cushion my mistakes if I made any. So I joined a driving school. At this point I knew how to ride a motorcycle so I had some idea about gears. The first day of driving school and the instructor made me drive out his Wagon R out of the city and onto the highway. This seemed like a good sign. I thought it meant I was doing good. And I honestly felt I was actually doing good. I was comfortable and not nervous. Back home I looked at my Alto. Excitedly, I just got into it and started messing around with the dials and the knobs trying to see what they do. I cranked the engine, put it into gear and moved it forward a few inches. Then I reversed it back into its original spot. It was very exciting. Being in a car all alone with no instructor, not exactly driving it but at least making it move.

After a few more lessons with the instructor in his Wagon R and with my friend in my Alto, I was confident practising on empty city roads late in the night. I had gotten the hang of it. Eventually, I gave my driver's license test, passed it and got my license. Initially I really liked driving but as with everything the high faded a little with traffic and issues finding parking. The car needed some things fixed as well from time to time. But overall it was a good experience. I learned a lot with this car and it was extremely useful in my family as this was the only way I could drive my dad around town. We even took a few trips out of town to my village. Overall the car served us well and I had a lot of fun driving it.

## To buy or not to buy

A few years down the line, my parents thought we should buy a new car. I was mostly against the idea. The Alto we had was good enough for our purpose. A decent upgrade would cost us around 8 to 10 lakhs at least. That was a lot of money. Not to mention insurance, service and other running costs. Being the only person in the family who could drive, I was already in charge of maintaining a scooter, my motorcycle and the Alto. The Alto did have some issues from time to time but it wasn't too bad. This was a 2007/2008 Alto with around 60,000 km on the odo. It drove very well though. I didn't want the hassle of owning and maintaining a new car. Not to mention traffic and bad roads. Sure it would replace the Alto so that burden would decrease but still. A part of me did also think that a new car will have fewer issues so that would be nice. But again, spending that much to replace a car that had been fulfilling most of our needs didn't feel alright. So I avoided it for quite some time.

I changed my mind a few months down the line for a few reasons.

1. Car ownership in India is more than what it seems. For a middle class family, it's aspirational. More than me, it would make my parents happier to sit in a new car. They probably never bought a car because most of their income was spent my siblings' and my education. And now they're at a stage in life where they've toiled for years and now deserve comfort.

2. The more I learnt about cars the more I felt the Alto was unsafe. It had no airbags and no ABS. It was reliable enough to drive around town but with issues popping up every now and then, I stopped taking it out of the city cause of the fear of being left stranded. I had changed a lot of flats too. The record being three times in the same day and it was the same tyre too. The manual windows and locks were annoying as well. Sure it was safer that a scooter but it could be a lot safer and comfier. And with the number of accidents in India, you could never have enough safety.

With all of this in mind, I decided now was the time to get a new car.

## Choosing a car

My requirements for a car were simple - safe, small, reliable, easy to own and must cost around 10 lakhs. Safety was my highest priority. I wanted the car to be 5 star rated with 6 airbags and the entire suite of safety features like ABS, EBD and more. I would be driving this car in the city 90% of the time so I wanted it to be small. I wanted it to be reliable and easy to own since I do not like service center trips. And my budget was around 10 lakhs.

Initially I was only looking at hatchbacks since I wanted a small car. I started looking at websites to see what what cars fit my criteria. Though I didn't find the perfect fit online, I still decided to go and see what the market had to offer. I already had the Kylaq in mind but it was not out yet and test drives were not available.

I had never been actively looking for a car before this point in time but I had a positive image of Tata cars in my mind. Mostly because of them prioritizing safety when no one else was doing it in the Indian car market. I also really liked the design of the Altroz and the Tiago. The more I looked into Tata cars, the more they steered me away. My primary concern was their reliability and service. I had read terrible things about Tata's reliability and service on the internet. Also I did not like the idea of having a 3 cylinder NA engine. The reliability and service concerns were honestly enough for me to stay away from Tata. I skipped over Mahindra for the same reasons. In hindsight, I should have at least visited their showrooms and checked out their cars for a better comparison. Although a few months later, my friend bought a Tata Punch. I was with him during the entire process and the buying experience was absolutely terrible. There was literal shouting at one point. I visited Mahindra showrooms as well with my friend during his car hunt and the experience was decent.

I started with the Suzuki Ignis. I had a bad experience at two nexa showrooms and a decent experience at the third. But I loved how it looked and it drove fantastic. It was small, it had a peppy engine and it was in my budget. What pushed me away was the lack of safety features. It only had 2 airbags and its crash rating was abysmal. I really wanted to get a Maruti since they are easy to live with but I could not compromise on safety. I think the Ignis is highly underrated and will be remember fondly for its 4 cylinder engine once its gone. This is a car I could see myself having for years and years but it'll always be the one that got away.

Next I tried the i10 and the i20. I liked how the i10 drove but did not like the design. With the i20, I loved the design but did not like how it drove. The same engine that felt peppy in the i10 felt lethargic and underpowered in the i20. Eventually I skipped both cause of bad safety ratings. The showroom experience was decent here.

## Narrowing in on the Kylaq

This was just around the time when the Kylaq was announced but test drives were not available yet. I had not considered sub4m SUVs because I was initially only looking at small hatch backs. But the i20 is the same length as the Kylaq and other sub4m SUVs. The Kylaq seemed pretty good on paper. So I waited for the test drive. Happy to say, the test drive went very well. The showroom experience was good. I was able to take a long test drive and the salesperson also suggested we go on some bad roads to test the suspension. I loved how it drove. The engine was fantastic and the handling was incredible. The build quality felt incredible and unlike anything I had seen before. I had made up my mind just with the test drive. I went and booked a Kylaq the same evening.

I think I paid a booking amount of 15,000 rupees and made it clear that I would not pay anything else until I could do a PDI. The dealer agreed but did not allow for a third party PDI which was fine since I wanted to do it myself anyway. Since it was a new car and it demand, I got no discounts or free accessories. I had booked the Signature MT in the Lava Blue colour but a week or so later changed it to Red. I did compare my quote with other online and found that I was overpaying a lot for insurance. So I talked to the dealer a couple of times and got him to lower his initial quote buy around 50%. It still wasn't as low as getting the insurance from outside but I didn't wanna go through that hassle. The subsequent interactions with the dealer when negotiating were not as good as I had hoped. I had read people having much smoother deals. But I didn't have another Skoda dealer in my city and I had no other options for a car I liked. It wasn't too bad but it could have been easier. I waited around 2 months for my car, did the PDI myself, and got her home.

## Getting her home and journey to 5000 km

I got the delivery for my Kylaq on the 2nd of May 2025. I was super excited and drove around 100 km on the first day. Now having completed over 5000 km, I can share some thoughts on various aspects of this car.

I'll start with the positives. The power and handling of this car is unmatched. There is a little turbo lag but honestly I don't think its that bad. The turbo kicks in at around 2500 to 3000 rpm and the car pulls clean all the way to the red line. It feels stable and planted in the corners and in high speeds. The car feels roomy and spacious. The interiors are simple and clean. The controls are tactile and easy to use. I like that it has buttons and knobs for everything including the AC. The steering controls are easy to use and intuitive. There's a decent amount of storage in the cabin including under the infotainment, under the arm rest, the cooled glove box and door pockets. All doors have storage pockets big enough for 1 litre water bottles. The seats are comfy and all 5 passenger seats have head rests. It has been reliable and I have had no real issues with the car. I usually drive it in traffic but I've done a few out of city trips. This car truly shines on the highway. I used to read about downshifting to overtake on the highways and I even do it sometimes on my motorcycle. But on the Kylaq I've rarely needed to do it. The car pulls clean in all gears. The boot is really spacious. On a particular long trip, I had a total of 5 people in the car, including myself, the boot was completely packed with luggage and the car didn't break a sweat all day. The suspension is well tuned and I've never had any issues with it bottoming out. It does a fantastic job of absorbing road undulations providing stability at the same time. 


Now some downsides. While the long travel of the clutch is not really an issue, the car jerks forward aggressively when you go from neutral to the 1st gear unless you release the clutch real slow. The problem I've noticed it that near the bite point, the rpms jumps from idle(1000) to 1200 for a second and then comes back down to a 1000. This cause the car to leap unless you release the clutch slow enough for the rpms to normalize. Or you could just match the rpms by revving it a little. The car does go off the line smoothly like this but it gains a lot of speed and this only works when the roads are fairly empty and definitely doesn't work for traffic. You have to release the clutch slow in traffic. One other think that works is releasing the clutch a bit further than the bite point, letting it go to 1200 rpm and then immediately depressing the clutch again and wait for the rpms to come down to 1000. Now when you release the clutch car has caught some speed and it won't jerk forward. I do not know if releasing this way is good for the clutch mechanism but this is just my observation. This has been a little annoying. I thought it was just me not being familiar enough with a powerful car and that with enough experience it'd get better and it has but still every now and then it jerks forward. All of this happens when the AC is turned on. When the AC is off, the car's behaviour changes. The car just feels a lot more relaxed and open. Like nothing is holding it back. And when you turn the AC on, it feels like something is restricting the engine. I didn't think a car this powerful would have such an effect from the AC stealing some power but it is there. Speaking of the AC, it is subpar to decent at best. In the peak Indian summer when the temperature crosses 45 degrees celsius, the AC struggles to cool the cabin. It does do a fair job but takes a long time and leaves something to be desired. This is a known issues of the Skoda/VW 1.0 TSI engine cars.

The infotainment has connection issues sometimes. 99% of the time I just need music and for that bluetooth should be enough but sometimes it just refuses to connect. I've added a dongle for wireless android auto and that has been working well for me. It connects 99% of the time automatically as long as my phone's bluetooth is turned on before I turn on the car. The screen itself is decent. It does lag a little while scrolling in Android Auto but nothing too bad. I mostly only need music and maps from the infotainment and the dongle I added that turns my wired android auto to wireless has been great. One gripe I have is that there is not volume knob in the center console. The only way to control the volume is through the touch screen or the steering controls. The steering controls work fine but I would have liked if there was a knob in the middle so that the co-passenger could control the volume. 

Now some things that I'm neutral about. I haven't really been seated in the rear of my car but it seems like the rear is mostly for 2 regular adults or 3 slim people. I did drive around 10 hours in a day with 3 of my friends in the back and they seemed decently comfortable. They have a slim build so that might have helped. The arm rest does move but in some seat positions does not move far enough to the front I feel. The first gear sometimes has trouble slotting in. The C pillar is thick causing a blind spot but I think that might need to be thick for structural integrity so all good. One thing that was mildly annoying but I have gotten used to is the wiper and the indicator stalks being in opposite positions than virtually every other Indian car. I got used to it fairly quick so nothing too bad. The light controls are not on the stalks but rather on the right of the steering wheel with a dedicated dial. The headlight throw seems decent enough especially in the city. Fuel economy in the city hovers around 8 to 9 kmpl which seems fair considering I usually drive in traffic and the amount of power this car makes plus its weight. On the highway the fuel economy increases drastically. I've gotten anywhere from 12 to 17 kmpl on longer trips. The height adjust for the driver slowly goes down after a few days and I have to pump it up every now and then.

I understand some downsides of my car might be solved with a higher trim level like Signature+ or Prestige. But with a higher trim, there are some cons too, like having a sunroof which affects structural integrity. Signature+/Prestige also have more complicated AC control and chrome treatment here and there which I am not a fan of. There are some things that I miss from the higher trims. Like a rear view camera, a grab handle for co-passenger, built in wireless android auto, a rear wiper, and ventilated seats.

The car has been dead reliable for the 5000 km I've driven it. Its not a lot of km though so only time will tell how this fares. The longest trip I've done with my car was from Vadodara to Jamnagar and back. The car performed exceptionally well with a full boot, 5 passengers including myself and still managed to give a fuel economy of 17 kmpl which I think was fantastic.
